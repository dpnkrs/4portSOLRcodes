Chapter 5

Experimental Validation of Four-Port SOLR
Calibration

   Chapter 3 established the experimental foundation for the four-port measurement
and validation work that follows by introducing the THz measurement facility, the four-
port measurement hardware, the instrument-side calibration hierarchy, and the practical
limitations of existing on-chip calibration approaches. Building on that measurement
background, Chapter 4 developed the four-port SOLR framework itself, including the
error-model basis, the design of the one-port and reciprocal standards, and the defi-
nition of the on-chip standard plane after removal of the common probing pads and
access network. The present chapter turns from that design-stage and methodology-
stage development to measurements on the taped-out calibration die. The chapter aims
to determine whether the extracted one-port standards are sufficiently well behaved, re-
peatable, and distinct to support a stable measured SOLR calibration over the intended
frequency range.
   A central point of the experimental validation is that the Chapter 4 EM and virtual-
mTRL results are retained here as design references, but not as absolute ground-truth
baselines for the fabricated standards. At mmWave and D-band frequencies, calibrated
on-wafer results are known to depend on probe geometry, touchdown conditions, neigh-
boring structures, substrate environment, and reference-plane placement [61,70,73]. For
that reason, the quality of the fabricated standards is assessed here primarily through
measurement-led criteria: physical plausibility of the extracted responses, separation
among the standards on the Smith Chart complex reflection plane, repeatability across
redundant realizations, conditioning of the resulting SOL solution matrix, and the effect
of the extracted standards on the downstream calibration itself [83, 86, 89]. Simulation
is used later in the section as a secondary trend check, but not as the sole measure of
correctness.

5.1     Measurement and Characterization of the S/O/L Cal-
        ibration Standards
   This section presents the experimental characterization of the fabricated Short,
Open, and Load standards after removal of the common on-die embedding between
the probe pads and the intended standard-definition plane. Rather than treating the
standards as ideal objects whose quality can be judged only by direct comparison to
simulation, they are treated here as measured and extracted calibration standards. The
relevant question is therefore not whether the fabricated responses perfectly reproduce
the Chapter 4 design curves, but whether the extraction yields physically plausible,
repeatable, and sufficiently distinct one-port definitions at the chosen on-chip reference
plane to support a stable SOLR calibration. This interpretation is consistent with prior
on-wafer metrology work showing that calibrated results depend not only on nominal
standard design, but also on probe geometry, touchdown conditions, neighboring struc-
tures, and the exact placement of the reference plane [61, 70, 73].

multiplication, which makes extraction and de-embedding of the common embedding
substantially more natural than in ordinary S-parameter form [101–103]. Under the
adopted symmetry assumption, the left and right embedding halves are represented by

where Tthru,meas (f ) is the scattering-transfer matrix of the measured straight Thru,

the scattering-transfer matrix of the explicit center-line section of the straight Thru. In
the final extraction baseline, TL (f ) is constructed from the physical 25.44 µm center
section, while the remaining pads, vias, and local transitions are absorbed into TE (f ).
   The transmission behavior of the embedding is identified from the measured straight
Thru together with the explicit 25.44 µm line section. After the initial straight-Thru
factorization and bandwise line-model fitting, the de-embedded Thru responses were
largely well behaved, but the de-embedded Open and Short remained the main phys-
ical concern. However, the measured straight Thru may not, by itself, be sufficiently
constraining the reflective behavior at the two sides of the embedding network. In
the reflective-refinement stage, the embedding is updated selectively rather than re-
estimated in a fully unconstrained way. The transmission terms of the embedding
remain anchored to the straight-Thru-derived baseline, while the reflective terms are
adjusted using the measured Open, Short, and Load responses. The refinement is
guided by physically motivated criteria: the extracted Open and Short should remain
strongly reflective and near the unit circle, the extracted Load should remain compar-
atively small, the updated embedding should remain smooth across frequency, and the
final result should stay close to the original thru-derived baseline unless the measured
SOL data provides clear evidence to move it.

5.1.2   Evaluation Framework for the Extracted Standards
   Because the standards are obtained through a measurement-led extraction rather
than by direct ideal assignment, their quality is assessed here through the following
complementary criteria:

  1. Physical plausibility. The extracted standards should remain physically inter-
     pretable at the adopted reference plane. The Open and Short are expected to
     remain strongly reflective and to follow smooth capacitive and inductive trajec-
     tories, respectively, on the Smith chart. The Load should remain near the chart
     center over the usable frequency range, with positive real impedance and limited
     reactance [61, 70].

  2. Separation and identifiability. The Open, Short, and Load must remain suf-
     ficiently distinct in the complex reflection plane so that the one-port SOL system
     remains well posed. If the extracted Load drifts too close to either reflective
     standard, or if the Open and Short collapse toward similar trajectories, the solved
     error terms become increasingly sensitive to measurement noise and de-embedding
     error [103, 104].

  3. Repeatability across redundant realizations. Since the one-port standards
     were measured redundantly from opposite sides of the embedding, the spread
     among those extractions becomes part of the calibration-quality assessment rather
     than a secondary detail. Small spread indicates that the extracted standard is pri-
     marily determined by the physical structure, while large spread indicates residual
     sensitivity to embedding uncertainty, touchdown variation, or model incomplete-
     ness [61, 73].

  4. Extraction consistency. The extracted standards should remain consistent with
     the underlying thru-derived embedding model. In practice, this means that reflec-
     tive refinement should improve the physical plausibility of the Open, Short, and
     Load without materially degrading the transmission behavior already established
     by the straight Thru [101, 103].

of the usable band. The central experimental question is not whether either standard

5.1.5    Adopted One-Port Standards for the Measured SOLR Calibra-
         tion
   Based on the analyses above, the relevant outcome for the remainder of the chapter
is not whether the fabricated standards are ideal, but whether the extracted one-port
definitions are sufficiently trustworthy for the measured SOLR calibration to proceed
with confidence. The Open and Short must remain strongly reflective, smooth, and
distinct, while the Load must remain sufficiently central and repeatable to keep the
one-port system well posed. In this sense, the practical upper frequency limit of the
one-port kit is determined by the measured Load behavior together with the growth of
κSOL (f ) and the closure residual, rather than by simulation alone [61, 103, 104].
   For the downstream SOLR implementation, the accepted bandwise standards are re-
tained as the primary characterized one-port definitions. The stitched full-span curves
are retained as complementary reporting products and as convenient continuous ref-
erence views across bands. They are not interpreted as exact replacements for the
accepted bandwise measurements. Likewise, any residual mild non-passivity in the ac-
cepted extracted standards is retained as part of the measured characterization and
carried forward transparently.
